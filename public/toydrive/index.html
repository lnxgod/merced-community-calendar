      Why I started the toy drive

      Will Hatzer

      I was five on Christmas morning in 1986. I came downstairs and there was my first bicycle. It was blue. Money was tight for my parents that year, but all I knew that morning was that I had a bike.

      Years later, my parents told me how it got there. People we’d never met had donated through our local firehouse. I cleaned up that same bike and gave it to the firehouse so another kid could wake up to it on Christmas.

      My first toy drive was at a firehouse in Columbus, Ohio. I asked friends and family to pitch in. In 2010, our local Subaru club helped collect toys and coats. Businesses joined in, too. When I moved to California in 2014, I flew back to Ohio for one last Christmas drive there. Our family carried the tradition on here.

      Many of you helped us when we went by Team Charity Case. We’ve since started GameChangers AI, our 501(c)(3) nonprofit, to help kids and adults learn how to use AI.

      The drives through the years

      2015–2019

      In 2015 and 2016, Will’s employer matched donations dollar for dollar. From 2017 through 2019, our family bought the toys ourselves and dropped them off.

      2020

      $7,728.08 spent on toys

      Friends and supporters gave $7,716.85 to the drive.

      2021

      $1,992.46 spent on toys

      People gave $1,982 to our drive and $1,290 directly to Toys for Tots. That Christmas we tried syncing our lights to music for the first time, hoping to make the display part of a future drive.

      2022

      Over $2,000 worth of toys delivered

      Our kids traded in some of their own games to help buy a gift. Supporters also gave $2,110 directly to Toys for Tots.

      2023

      $2,567.62 spent on toys

      We received $895 for the drive. Donors gave another $960 directly to Toys for Tots.

      2024

      At least $4,213.66 in toy purchases

      Our first pickup brought in over $800 worth of donated toys. Local businesses contributed raffle prizes for the drive.
