Christmas Light Show

How we made the show

We used ChatGPT from the first light map through the last animation. The setup that used to eat up hours finally gave us time to make the show better.

From a light map to a finished song

Map the real lights

We gave ChatGPT our fixture inventory and described what was actually in the yard. It helped translate the rooflines, fence, arches, candy canes, trees, sign, and singing faces into xLights models. Each model needed the right channels and pixel order so an effect in xLights reached the right lights in the yard.

Bring the older shows back

We recovered sequences and matching recordings from past shows. ChatGPT helped match their old props to our current hardware and compare the rendered output with the originals. That meant we could keep the original mega-tree choreography on family favorites while giving the newer lights a part.

Give songs new animation

Some songs we wanted to use had no animation for the tree or mouth cues for the singers. We used ChatGPT to help build timed pixel scenes and line up the words with four separate singing faces. We could decide who took each verse and when the others should react.

Preview the whole house

We turned a street-level photo and a narrated walk-through into a house silhouette. With the real rooflines, windows, and yard props in the xLights preview, we could spot a misplaced strand or an arch that missed a cue before taking the show outside.

More time for the parts people see

Previous seasons meant hours tracing mappings and fixing imports, and the preview still didn’t quite match the house. This year we could spend that time choosing what the tree should show, which face should sing next, and how a musical phrase should pass across the yard. It’s the kind of practical, hands-on AI work we started GameChangers AI to teach.
